# Badminton Notes – user guide

Badminton Notes is for coaches and parents who want to follow a junior singles match and keep tactical notes. It runs in the browser and works offline. There is no account and nothing is sent anywhere.

## Starting a match

1. Open the app and press **+ New match**.
2. Enter the two player names and the date.
3. Choose the rules:
   - **Points per game** (21, 15, 11, 7 or any number).
   - **Time limit per game** in minutes (0 for none). A timer counts down and shows TIME UP.
   - **Point cap** (0 for none) and **Must win by 2 points**.
   - **Number of games** (best of). Use 1, 3 or 5.
4. Press **Start match**. You can change all of this later under ⚙ → **Names & rules**.

## Recording points

The court shows each player's half. Player 1 starts on the left. Draw with a finger, pen or mouse.

**Draw the path of the shuttle** from the first hit to where it lands.

- Each time the line turns back towards the net, the player on that side hit the shuttle. This is how returns are recorded, and also how a faulty return is caught.
- The last hit decides the point:
  - It crosses the net and lands in court: the last hitter wins the point.
  - It lands out, wide, or does not reach the net: the last hitter makes a fault and the other player gets the point.
- Each stretch of the line takes the colour of the player who hit it. Faults are dashed and end with a ✕. A dot marks each hit.
- **Tap** a half to record a fault by the player on that half (for example a service fault).
- Touches outside the court do nothing, so you can still scroll and zoom.

### Top view and side view

The **Top | Side** switch is in the middle of the score bar.

- **Top**: the court seen from above.
- **Side**: across is the court length, up is the height (net at about 1.75 m on screen). Draw the arc of the shuttle. Landing in the red OUT zones at the ends, or in the red strip under the court (wide), is a fault.

A point belongs to the view where it was drawn. Each view shows only its own points. The score counts all of them.

### Shot tags

After a point, tap a tag under the court (Smash, Drop, Clear and so on) to say how the rally ended. A tag applies to the last point. Tap it again to remove it.

### Correcting the score

- **Undo** removes the last point.
- The faint **−** and **+** next to each score change the score by one. Each change is logged.
- ⚙ → **Edit result** overwrites the total for a game and lets you pick the winner yourself. Use this when not every rally was recorded.
- ⚙ shows the full point list. You can delete any entry there.

## Games and sides

- When a game ends a banner appears with **Start game N**. When a player has won most of the games the banner announces the match winner.
- Sides alternate between games. **⇄** swaps the sides at any time. Earlier arrows swap with the players.
- ⚙ lets you switch between games of the match.

## Several matches

Tick the matches in the left list and open **Compare**. Each match shows game scores, the arrows in top view and side view, a stats table (points won with a shot, the opponent's faults, own faults, shot tags) and the notes. You can show all arrows, winners only or faults only.

Write notes for a match under ⚙ → **Match notes**.

## Full screen

**⛶ Full screen** hides the header and gives the court the whole screen. Use **✕ Exit** in the corner or the Esc key to leave. **☰** opens the match list. On phones that do not support full screen the page hides its header instead.

## Saving and sharing

Matches are saved automatically in this browser. They do not move between browsers or devices.

**Save / Load** lets you:

- export all matches, or only the ticked ones, as a JSON file (a full backup);
- export the points as a CSV file (one row per point, with coordinates and heights in centimetres);
- import a JSON file to restore or merge matches.

Clearing the browser's site data deletes your matches, so export a backup after important matches.
